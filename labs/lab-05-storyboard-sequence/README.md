# Lab 05 — Storyboard Sequence

**Course:** Canva Design Masterclass (`C156`)
**Version:** v1.0 · 30 September 2026

## Scenario

Plan a six-frame heat-exhaustion awareness video from risk recognition to action.

## Objective

Create an annotated storyboard that communicates a short visual narrative to collaborators.

## Supplied image files

- `story-reference-07.png`
- `story-reference-08.png`
- `story-reference-09.png`
- `story-reference-10.png`
- `story-reference-11.png`
- `story-reference-12.png`

## Detailed procedure

1. Write the hook, risk, symptoms, response and call-to-action as a six-beat outline.
2. Create a six-frame storyboard grid with consistent numbering and annotation zones.
3. Assign one communication objective to each frame.
4. Place or crop the supplied image references to create visual continuity.
5. Add concise on-screen copy, duration and transition notes for each frame.
6. Check that the sequence includes context, tension, resolution and action.
7. Ask a partner to reorder the frame summaries without seeing the numbers; fix ambiguous transitions.
8. Export the annotated storyboard as a high-resolution JPEG.

## Evidence to retain

Six-frame annotated storyboard JPEG plus sequence-test note.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each frame has one clear purpose.
- [ ] The narrative progresses logically to the call-to-action.
- [ ] Annotations specify timing or transition intent.
- [ ] A collaborator can understand the sequence without verbal explanation.

## Reflection prompts

- Which decision most improved the message or focal hierarchy?
- Which visible design decision most improved the audience's understanding?
- What source, permission or provenance evidence must accompany the finished image?
